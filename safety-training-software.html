The problem

Good training stuck in a format only you can deliver.

Most safety trainers and consulting firms have years of PowerPoint decks: scaffold inspection, fall protection, ladders, lockout, confined space. They work well in a classroom with you at the front. They don't work on their own. Without your voice, a learner gets bullet points and none of the explanation that lives in your speaker notes.

The usual fixes are slow. Recording yourself over every slide takes days and has to be redone every time a slide changes. Rebuilding the deck in an eLearning authoring tool takes longer. Buying an off-the-shelf course means your clients get generic content instead of yours.

How it works

From PowerPoint to eLearning in five steps.

  1. Upload your decks

    Drop in as many .pptx files as the course needs, in the order you teach them. The originals are kept with the course, so you can always go back to the source.

  2. Pick a length

    Tell it how long the course should run. Narration is budgeted across the modules to fit, and no single slide runs longer than about 90 seconds, so learners keep moving.

  3. Let it build

    Each deck becomes a module of short teaching slides. Each slide gets a picture and a narrated script written from the slide and your speaker notes, read in two alternating voices so a long course doesn't drone. The build runs in the background; leave the page and come back.

  4. Review the draft

    The draft opens with a list of where to look first, such as slides that had no speaker notes. Every slide shows the deck and source slide it came from. Edit any text or script, re-record one slide's audio, replace a picture, split, merge, reorder, or delete slides, and rename or reorder modules.

  5. Send for review, then publish

    Send your client a review link to page through the course without an account. When it's right, publish it to your learners as a self-paced course with completion tracking built in.

Questions

What trainers ask first.

Can I turn a PowerPoint safety deck into an online course?

Yes. Upload one or more .pptx decks and Safely Track builds a draft course: each deck becomes a module of short teaching slides, each with a picture and a narrated script written from the slide and your speaker notes. You edit the draft, send it for review, and publish it with completion tracking.

Does it use my speaker notes?

Yes. Speaker notes are where most trainers keep what they actually say, so the narration is written from them along with the slide text. Slides that had no notes are flagged so you know where to look first.

How long does a build take?

Minutes, not weeks. In our production testing a 125-slide course built in about 12 minutes. The build runs in the background, so you can leave the page and come back to the draft.

Can I control how long the course runs?

Yes. Set a target length before you build and the narration is budgeted across the modules to fit it. No single slide runs longer than about 90 seconds of narration.

Can a client review it before it goes live?

Yes. Send a review link and your client can page through the draft without creating an account or logging in.

Is our training content used to train AI models?

No. Decks are processed through the OpenAI API, which does not use API data to train its models. Your source decks are kept in your account so you can always go back to the original.
